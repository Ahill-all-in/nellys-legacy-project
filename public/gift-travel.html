Danell's Journey › Gifting travel

So you want to send us somewhere.

People keep offering me miles and points, and then we both get stuck, because every airline and hotel does this differently and half of them charge you for the privilege of being generous. So Austin went and figured it out. This is the whole thing in plain English — what costs nothing, what costs more than it is worth, and the one option almost nobody thinks of.

And if you are another patient or caregiver who wandered in here looking for help with travel yourself, scroll to the bottom. There is a whole list of people who will fly you and house you for free, and I wish somebody had handed it to me on day one.

The Short Version

Four ways in, best first

If you only read one thing on this page, read the dark box.

1. Best: book the trip yourself, in our names

Every major US airline — Delta, United, American, Southwest, Alaska/Atmos, JetBlue — lets you spend your own miles on a ticket for someone who is not you. No fee. No transfer cap. Nothing lost in the middle. Same for hotel rooms on all four big chains: book it, put my name on it as the guest, done.

It is the single most efficient way to do this, and it is the one most people don't realize is allowed.

2. Transfer hotel points

Hilton, Marriott and Hyatt all let you move points to another member's account for free. If you have a hotel balance sitting there, this one is genuinely painless and it gives us flexibility on dates. Details below.

Often overlooked

3. Offer a place, not points

A timeshare week you are not going to use. A rental with a gap between bookings. A condo, a cabin, a spare room near the water. Lodging is the expensive half of any trip, and this costs you nothing but a week on a calendar.

Always works

4. Gift card or cash

Airline and hotel gift cards are transferable and they cover the taxes and fees that award tickets don't. Plain cash covers gas, parking, bad hospital-cafeteria coffee and the dog sitter, which is usually the bigger number anyway.

The One Nobody Thinks Of

A week somewhere beats a pile of points

If you own or manage a timeshare, a vacation rental, a beach condo, a cabin, or you just have a place near the water that sits empty part of the year — that is the most valuable thing on this entire page, and it costs you nothing out of pocket.

1

Tell us roughly what and when. Where it is, how many it sleeps, and which weeks are actually open. We are flexible — I am not picky about the month, and off-season is honestly easier for us anyway.

2

Timeshare owners: most systems let you deposit or gift a week, or add a guest certificate so someone else can check in under your reservation. Marriott Vacation Club, Hilton Grand Vacations, Wyndham, Bluegreen and the exchange networks (RCI, Interval International) all have some version of this. Your member services line will know the exact form in about four minutes.

3

Rental owners: a blocked week handed to us directly is simplest — no platform, no fees, no service charge. If you would rather run it through Airbnb or Vrbo for the insurance, that works too.

4

Near a transplant center? Different kind of gift, same idea. If you have a spare room or an in-law suite within driving distance of a major cancer center, there are families sleeping in hospital chairs right now who would take that in a heartbeat. Hospitality Homes and the Healthcare Hospitality Network both place hosts. Tell them, not us.

Airline Miles

Please book the flight. Don't transfer the miles.

I know that sounds ungrateful. Stay with me — the math is genuinely bad, and it is bad for you, not for me.

Most airlines charge you real money to move miles into somebody else's account. Delta wants a penny a mile plus thirty dollars. Moving 30,000 SkyMiles — roughly one domestic round trip — costs you $330. Those same 30,000 miles are worth about $300 to $350 in actual airfare. You would be paying nearly the full value of the gift for permission to give it away.

If you spend your miles on a ticket with my name on it instead, that fee is zero and I get every mile's worth. Same generosity, none of the leakage.

What we need from you to book a flight

Legal first and last name exactly as it appears on the ID we travel with, date of birth, our home airport, and a rough window. That's it. Add our own frequent flyer number to the reservation so we still earn the credit, and know that any changes or cancellations have to come from you, since you own the booking.

Hotel Points

Hotels are the opposite. Transfer away.

Three of the four big chains move points between members for free. If you have a hotel balance, this is the easy yes.

One nice trick, if you're the type

Hilton runs a 100% bonus on purchased points fairly often, which lands the price around half a cent a point — right about what they're worth on redemption. Since Hilton transfers are free, someone who wanted to could buy during a promo and pass them straight along at no extra cost. We are not asking anyone to spend money on this. But people ask, so: that's the answer.

Things Nobody Tells You

Four honest notes before you do anything

Sorry

None of this is tax-deductible

Donated miles are treated by the IRS as a gift from the airline to the recipient, not from you — even when the recipient is a registered charity. And a gift to a family is a personal gift, which is never deductible in any form. If the write-off matters to you, give to a 501(c)(3) instead. That is a real and good thing to do, it just isn't this.

We looked

There is no points-transfer website

People keep asking whether there's a site where a family posts a request and strangers send miles. There isn't. United's Miles on a Mission comes closest, but campaigns are limited to vetted nonprofits, not individuals. Miles4Migrants, which used to run something similar, shut down. So it really is email and a booking — sorry, that's just where the world is.

Please don't

Skip the mileage brokers

Sites that buy and sell miles violate every airline's terms of service, and the account that gets shut down and stripped is yours, not ours. Same goes for anything that looks like a trade — if you give miles and get something back, even a thank-you gift, that's technically bartering. Keep it one-way and you're fine.

Worth knowing

Points expire quietly

Marriott, Hilton, IHG and Hyatt all wipe balances after roughly 24 months of no activity, and most airline programs do something similar. If you've been sitting on a balance you forgot about, it may be closer to the cliff than you think. Transferring or spending them resets the clock.

If You're the Patient

People who will fly you and house you for free

This part isn't about us. If you are a cancer or transplant patient staring down travel you cannot afford, these organizations exist and most people have never heard of a single one of them. Almost all of them are easier to reach through your treatment center's social worker than on your own — several of them require it. Ask for that person by name. They are the most underused resource in the entire hospital.

Start Here

Corporate Angel Network

Free seats on empty corporate jets to treatment. They explicitly cover bone marrow and stem cell transplant patients and donors, and — this is the unusual part — there is no financial need requirement. You just have to be ambulatory and not need in-flight medical care. They have flown over 70,000 patients.

Free Lodging

ACS Hope Lodge

Free rooms for patients and a caregiver, at 30+ locations near major treatment centers. The American Cancer Society also partners with Extended Stay America for tens of thousands of free and discounted nights a year. Both go through ACS — call the 24-hour line at 1-800-227-2345.

Transplant Patients

NMDP Patient Assistance

The registry that found my donor also has a financial assistance program covering travel, temporary housing, food and gas for the patient and a caregiver. Note that your transplant center's staff has to submit it — you can't apply directly, so bring it up at your next appointment.

Ask Anyway

Your hospital's own hotel list

Nearly every large cancer center quietly negotiates a "patient rate" with hotels nearby that is far below anything you'll find online, and they almost never advertise it. Ask the social worker or the front desk for the medical-rate list. This is the single highest-yield, least-known move on this page.

Two more worth knowing about: PAN Foundation offers a $500 transportation grant, though you need an active PAN grant already, and Blood Cancer United (formerly the Leukemia & Lymphoma Society) runs a travel assistance fund that opens and closes through the year — worth checking even if it's closed today, because it reopens.
